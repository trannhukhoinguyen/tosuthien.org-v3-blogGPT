In Mahayana Buddhist teachings (such as the Mahaparinirvana Sutra), just before Shakyamuni Buddha entered final parinirvana (complete Nirvana), he moved through the nine stages of meditative absorption (jhāna/dhyāna) in forward and reverse order, and then delivered profound final guidance and warnings to the assembly of monks and disciples regarding the true nature of emptiness, the Dharma, and liberation.

<blockquote>

## The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra (Latter Part)

Chapter Two: Returning to the Source When What Should Be Exhausted Is  The Buddha again addressed the great assembly: "My time has now come;
At that time, before His words had even faded, the World-Honored One 

At that time, having thus entered the various dhyānas in forward and reverse order, the World-Honored One universally addressed the great assembly: "With profound Prajñā[^1], I have universally surveyed all six destinies within the Three Realms, along with all mountains, great oceans, and all sentient beings of the great earth. 
Such Three Realms are fundamentally detached in their essential nature, ultimately quiescent and extinguished, identical to the mark of empty space. 
Without name and without consciousness, having forever severed all states of existence (諸有), they are originally equal, devoid of notions of high or low. 
Without seeing, without hearing, without sensing, and without knowing, they can neither be bound nor liberated. 
There are no sentient beings and no lifespan; there is no birth, no arising, no exhaustion, and no extinction.
"Neither worldly nor unworldly, neither Nirvāṇa nor saṃsāra can be apprehended, for both extremes are equal.
Because all dharmas are equal, one dwells in serene stillness without deliberate action, and ultimate establishment cannot be attained. 
Acting in accordance with Dharma-nature from the standpoint of non-abiding dharmas, one severs all marks so that not a single thing exists. Such is the true mark of dharmas. 
Whoever knows this is called a transcendent person (出世人); not knowing this is called the beginning of saṃsāra. 
All of you in the great assembly ought to sever ignorance and extinguish the beginning of saṃsāra."

At that time, having spoken these words, the World-Honored One again entered the Transcendental Dhyānas (超禪 - leaping dhyānas[^2]): 

emerging from the First Dhyāna, He entered the Third Dhyāna; emerging from the Third Dhyāna, He entered the Sphere of Boundless Space; emerging from Boundless Space, He entered the Sphere of Nothingness; emerging from Nothingness, He entered the Samādhi of Cessation. 
Emerging from the Samādhi of Cessation, He entered in reverse order into the Sphere of Neither Perception nor Non-Perception; emerging from Neither Perception nor Non-Perception, He entered the Sphere of Boundless Consciousness; emerging from Boundless Consciousness, He entered the Fourth Dhyāna; emerging from the Fourth Dhyāna, He entered the Second Dhyāna; emerging from the Second Dhyāna, He entered the First Dhyāna.
Having thus entered the Transcendental Dhyānas in forward and reverse order, He again addressed the great assembly: "With Mahāprajñā, I have universally surveyed all sentient and insentient things within the Three Realms. 
All persons and dharmas are completely ultimate—there is neither one who is bound nor one who is liberated. 
Without a master and without a support, they cannot be grasped or held. 
They neither exit the Three Realms nor enter into the various states of existence. 
Originally pure, undefiled, and free from afflictions, they are equal to empty space—neither equal nor unequal. 
Exhausting all stirring thoughts, the conceptualizing mind comes to rest. Such a mark of dharmas is called Mahāparinirvāṇa. 
Truly seeing this Dharma is called liberation; ordinary beings who do not know it are said to be in ignorance."
Having spoken these words, He again entered the Transcendental Dhyānas, ascending from the First Dhyāna up to the Samādhi of Cessation, and descending from the Samādhi of Cessation back to the First Dhyāna. 
Having thus entered the Transcendental Dhyānas in forward and reverse order, He again addressed the great assembly: "With the Buddha-Eye, I have universally surveyed all dharmas within the Three Realms. 
The fundamental limit of ignorance is intrinsically liberated by nature; sought throughout the ten directions, it cannot be found anywhere.
Because the root is non-existent, all dependent branches and leaves are likewise liberated. 
Because ignorance is liberated, everything down to old age and death attains liberation. For this reason, I now abide peacefully in the Light of Eternal Quiescence and Extinction (常寂滅光), which is called Mahāparinirvāṇa."

At that time, Ānanda was overwhelmed with boundless grief, sorrow, and agony. 
His mind was frantic and confused, his faculties clouded as if heavily intoxicated, entirely devoid of awareness. 
He did not see the fourfold assembly, nor did he know whether the Tathāgata had already entered Nirvāṇa or had not yet entered Nirvāṇa. 
At that time, the World-Honored One thus entered the various dhyānas three times by leaping, universally surveyed the Dharma-realm, and expounded the Dharma three times for the great assembly. 
In this manner, the Tathāgata rotated through twenty-seven transitions in and out of the various dhyānas and samādhis. 
Because Ānanda did not know, each time the Buddha entered a dhyāna, Ānanda asked a question. 
Twenty-seven times he asked Aniruddha: "Has the Buddha already entered Nirvāṇa, or has He not yet entered Nirvāṇa?" Aniruddha deeply knew that the Tathāgata was entering the various dhyānas and samādhis, and all twenty-seven times he replied to Ānanda: "The Buddha has not yet entered Nirvāṇa." 
At that time, the entire great assembly was utterly bewildered, none of them knowing whether the Tathāgata had entered Nirvāṇa or not.

Then, having entered the various dhyānas three times and instructed the assembly three times, the World-Honored One lay down on His right side upon the seven-jeweled couch, His head to the north, His feet pointing south, His face turned to the west, and His back to the east. 
That seven-jeweled couch was adorned with wondrous necklaces of gems. 
In the grove of Śāla trees, there were four pairs, eight trees in all—one pair to the west in front of the Tathāgata, one pair to the east behind the Tathāgata, one pair to the north at the Buddha's head, and one pair to the south at the Buddha's feet. 
At that time, beneath the Śāla grove, reclining upon the jeweled couch, in the middle of the night, the World-Honored One entered the Fourth Dhyāna, utterly silent without a sound. In that very instant, He entered Parinirvāṇa!

Once the Greatly Awakened World-Honored One had entered Nirvāṇa, the eastern and western pairs of trees in the Śāla grove merged into a single tree, and the northern and southern pairs merged into a single tree, drooping down over the jeweled couch to canopy the Tathāgata. 
Immediately, those trees turned grievously pale white, like white cranes; their branches, leaves, flowers, fruits, bark, and trunks all burst open and fell away, gradually withering and snapping until nothing remained.

At that time, the great earth throughout all Buddha worlds as numerous as the sands of countless tens of thousands of millions of Ganges Rivers in the ten directions quaked violently, emitting various sounds crying out: "Alas! Alas! The world is empty and bereft!"—proclaiming lamentations of impermanence, suffering, and emptiness. 
At that time, all mountains throughout the worlds of the ten directions—Mount Mucilinda, Mount Mahāmucilinda, the Iron-Ring Mountains (Cakravāḍa), the Great Iron-Ring Mountains, all Mount Sumerus, Fragrant Mountains, Jeweled Mountains, Golden Mountains, Black Mountains, and all mountains upon the great earth—cracked and collapsed simultaneously, roaring with a mighty sound that shook the worlds and cried out: "Alas! Alas! How has the world suddenly been left orphaned, with the Sun of Wisdom setting behind the Great Mountain of Nirvāṇa? All sentient beings have lost their true Compassionate Father, and gods and humans have lost the One they revere and look up to!" At that time, all great oceans throughout the worlds of the ten directions churned turbulently and boiled over with surging waves, emitting various sounds crying out: "Alas! Alas! The Perfectly Awakened One is now extinct! Sentient beings, burdened by offense and suffering, drift endlessly through the long night in the great ocean of saṃsāra; having lost the right path, how shall they ever find liberation?" At that time, all rivers, streams, ravines, torrents, springs, canals, wells, and bathing pools overturned and dried up completely, devoid of water.

At that time, the great earth and empty space throughout the worlds of the ten directions fell into profound, silent darkness; the radiance of the sun and moon ceased to shine, and gloom and sorrow pervaded the universe. 
At that very moment, a fierce black wind suddenly rose in fury, howling and shaking the earth, blowing dust and sand to darken the worlds. 
Thereupon, all grasses, medical herbs, trees, flowers, fruits, branches, and leaves upon the great earth snapped and shattered, falling to the ground without remainder.

At that moment, all devas throughout the worlds of the ten directions filled empty space, wailing and lamenting, shaking the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world system. 
They rained down countless hundreds of thousands of varieties of supreme heavenly incense and heavenly flowers, filling the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world system and piling up as high as Mount Sumeru as an offering to the Tathāgata. High in the sky, they also rained down countless heavenly banners, pennants, jeweled necklaces, canopies, and precious pearls, filling empty space and transforming into jeweled platforms draped on all four sides with strings of pearls and seven-jeweled nets, radiant with brilliant splendor, as an offering to the Tathāgata. High in the sky, they also played countless wondrous heavenly melodies with drums, winds, strings, and singing, emitting various sounds crying out: "Alas! Alas! The Buddha has entered Nirvāṇa! The world is empty and bereft, the eye of living beings is extinguished, the rākṣasas of affliction and great desire run rampant, and the suffering of conditioned existence continues unbroken as the wheel of pain turns without cease!"
